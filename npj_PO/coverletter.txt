Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript "PREDICT-AML: Personalized REsponse via Drug
Interaction with Cellular Traits for Acute Myeloid Leukemia" for consideration in npj
Precision Oncology. PREDICT-AML is a multi-omics machine learning framework for personalized
drug response prediction in AML, developed on the BeatAML cohort and externally validated on
two independent patient cohorts.

Prior approaches have been limited by single-cohort evaluation, inflated benchmarks from random
cross-validation, and restricted drug coverage. PREDICT-AML addresses all three gaps. The key
contributions are:

  * State-of-the-art performance with rigorous evaluation. TabPFN with Knowledge-Distilled
    drug embeddings achieves r_pc = 0.690 (MAE = 37.712) on the held-out BeatAML test set --
    surpassing the best published ElasticNet approach (r_pc = 0.360) and the MDREAM ensemble
    (r_sc = 0.685 vs. 0.680) across all 150 test drugs. We demonstrate that standard random
    cross-validation inflates apparent performance by ~13% relative to patient-stratified CV
    and establish the latter as the clinically appropriate benchmark.

  * Simultaneous multi-cohort external validation. PREDICT-AML is validated on LeeAML
    (r_pc = 0.597; 30 patients, 49 drugs) and FIMM-AML (|r_pc| = 0.557; 187 patients,
    78 drugs), spanning distinct institutions, geographic sites, and drug sensitivity platforms
    (AUC vs. DSS).

  * Four complementary drug representations (physicochemical, Knowledge-Distilled embeddings,
    ChemBERTa, MolFormer) augmented by patient-specific drug-pathway proximity scores derived
    from network propagation on the STRING protein-protein interaction network.

  * Novel reduced-panel finding. Somatic mutations combined with cell-state enrichment scores
    alone -- without gene expression -- recapitulate full multi-omics performance (r_sc = 0.685),
    offering a practical alternative for resource-limited clinical settings.

  * Novel biological discoveries. Across the top-10 predicted drugs, BCL6 expression is
    significant in all ten compounds across mechanistically distinct drug classes -- extending
    beyond its established role in FLT3 inhibitor resistance. Immunogenic cell death (ICD)
    pathway activity emerges as a novel multi-drug transcriptional biomarker of AML sensitivity.
    Monocyte-like cell-state enrichment confers Venetoclax resistance (beta = 0.77, R^2 = 0.87)
    while predicting MEK-inhibitor sensitivity, consistent with MCL-1 dependence in monocytic
    blasts.

  * SHAP interpretability recovers established mechanisms (IDH2/BCL-2 synthetic lethality) and
    identifies POU4F1 transcriptional state as a novel candidate sensitivity biomarker without
    explicit pathway supervision.

All code and workflows are publicly available at https://github.com/raghvendra5688/PT-AML2.0.
